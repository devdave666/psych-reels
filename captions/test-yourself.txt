Rereading your notes feels like learning. Testing yourself actually makes it stick.

In a 2006 study (Henry Roediger & Jeffrey Karpicke, Psychological Science), 180 college students learned a short science passage. One group read it four times and another read it once and then wrote down everything they could recall, three times. A week later the self-testers remembered 61% of the ideas versus 40% for the rereaders, even though the rereaders had gone through the passage about 14 times to their 3. Honest caveat: on a test just 5 minutes later rereading came out ahead (83% vs 71%), and this was a lab study with prose passages, but the long-term edge for self-testing has held up across many later studies.

Next time you study, close the book and write down what you remember before you look again.
