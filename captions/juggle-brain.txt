Think you're too old to learn something new? Your brain disagrees.
In a 2008 study (Boyke, Driemeyer, Gaser, Büchel & May, Journal of Neuroscience), 44 healthy adults aged 50 to 67 spent 3 months learning to juggle three balls while 25 people of the same age did not. Brain scans of the learners showed temporary increases in grey matter in an area that tracks visual motion and in the hippocampus, a key memory region, mirroring an earlier study of 20-year-old jugglers (Draganski et al., 2004, Nature). Honest caveats: it was a small MRI study, the older learners juggled less well than young adults (only 10 of 44 kept it going for a full minute), and the changes faded once they stopped practising, so the takeaway is that the brain responds to practice, not that juggling is a brain upgrade.
