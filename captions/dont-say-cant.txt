Trying to stick to a goal? Change one word: say "I don't", not "I can't".

In a 2012 paper (Vanessa Patrick & Henrik Hagtvedt, Journal of Consumer Research), 30 working women with health goals were told to turn down temptations with "I don't...", "I can't..." or just "no", and to report every day for 10 days for as long as it kept working. 8 of 10 in the "I don't" group kept going all 10 days, vs 1 of 10 in the "I can't" group (and 3 of 10 who just said no). In a lab study with 120 students, 64% of the "I don't" group picked a granola bar over chocolate vs 39% of the "I can't" group.

These are small studies, so treat it as a promising trick, not a law. "I can't" sounds like a rule someone gave you; "I don't" sounds like who you are. The candles in the video are illustrative, the final counts are from the paper.
