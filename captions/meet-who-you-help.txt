Running on empty? Go meet the person your work is for.

In a 2007 field experiment (Adam Grant and colleagues, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes), 39 paid callers at a university fundraising call centre were split into three groups. The 17 who spent about 5 minutes talking with a student whose scholarship their calls helped fund were tracked a month later: they spent 142% more time on the phone and raised 171% more money per week than before, while callers who only read the student's letter, or had no contact, did not change. Honest caveat: it was a small sample at one organisation, so treat it as a strong clue, not a guarantee.

Who does your work actually help? Find one of them this week and listen to their story.
