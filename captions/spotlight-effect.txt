Nobody is watching you as closely as you think.

In a classic study (Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky, 2000), students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room of their peers. They guessed that about half the room (46%) would notice it. Only about 23% actually did.

Psychologists call it the spotlight effect: you're the main character of your own life, so it feels like everyone else is watching you too. They're busy starring in their own.
