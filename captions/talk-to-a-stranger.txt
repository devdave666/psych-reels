Too awkward to talk to the stranger next to you? They probably want to talk more than you think.
In a 2014 study (Nicholas Epley & Juliana Schroeder, Journal of Experimental Psychology: General), Chicago-area train commuters were randomly asked to talk to a stranger, sit in solitude, or commute as usual. The 97 who returned surveys in the first experiment reported a more positive commute after connecting than after sitting alone, even though other commuters predicted the opposite. In a follow-up, commuters guessed fewer than 47% of people would be willing to talk, yet everyone who tried got a conversation and not one person reported being rebuffed. Honest caveat: these were self-reported moods from mailed-back surveys in one city, so treat it as a strong nudge, not a guarantee.
Tomorrow, say hello to one stranger: a neighbour on the train, the barista, the person in line.
