Feeling flat even on a good day? Check where your mind actually is.

In a 2010 study (Matthew Killingsworth & Daniel Gilbert, Science), an iPhone app pinged 2,250 adults at random moments to ask how they felt, what they were doing, and whether they were thinking about something else. Their minds were wandering in 46.9% of the samples, and people were less happy while wandering than while focused, during every activity, even the dullest ones. Drifting off to pleasant thoughts didn't make them any happier than staying with what they were doing. Honest caveats: it's self-reported data from people who chose to download a happiness app, and while the authors' timing analysis suggested wandering came before the low mood, other researchers argue it can run the other way too.

Pick one thing you do today and give it your whole attention, start to finish.
