Stuck on an idea? Stand up and walk.

In a 2014 Stanford study (Marily Oppezzo and Daniel Schwartz, Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition), 176 people, mostly college students, were tested in four experiments. Walking, on a treadmill or outdoors, boosted idea generation compared with sitting, and in one experiment walkers gave about twice as many creative responses. It only helped open-ended thinking, not single-answer problems, and it was a small lab study, so treat it as a handy trick rather than a guarantee.

Next time you're stuck, don't stare harder. Go for a walk.
